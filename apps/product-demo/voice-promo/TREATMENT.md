# Voice Closure — promo treatment

## Brief
An approximately 90-second, narrated, 1920×1080, 30 fps meeting promo for
THBS Field Assist. Focus exclusively on the engineer's voice journey and its
proposed integration with existing applications. Preserve the earlier full
product walkthrough. Output MP4, poster, English SRT, editable source and Player.
Use all nine requested Snapcn components as owned Remotion source.

## Structures considered
1. A whole working day: compelling but would imply unmeasured shift benefits.
2. A feature list: comprehensive but loses the engineer's story.
3. One spoken visit becoming one reviewable record: selected. The turn is the
   spoken measurement becoming a structured answer; the ending gives the
   engineer control and proposes a measured pilot.

## Direction
Use the Lemo-Opuscar directing workflow and Dark Tech Keynote grammar, adapted
to the explicitly requested Phone Frame, Portal Intro and Dot Bloom. Near-black
blue surfaces, one electric-violet accent, raised UI cards, generous empty space,
Inter and Inter Tight, no grain. The cream Dot Bloom is a brief intentional
transition; it is not a second product palette. No Openreach logo or endorsement.
The medium's signature move is speech fragments gathering into five field rows.

References teach timing and hierarchy only: precision typography and restrained
launch-film light reveals. No borrowed footage, designs, score or third-party
brand marks. No style demo source is needed for this original Remotion film.

## Shots and camera
1. Word Gather: words find their positions; locked wide with a purposeful push.
2. Portal Intro: a voice picture chip expands, then settles into a clean product
   lockup. The title background has no central artwork underneath the lettering.
3. Phone Frame + Screen Recording: illustrative synthetic capture, vertical
   screen source; a locked readable capture inside the moving device frame.
4. Missing-only question: framed phone, question / answer / review, restrained cut.
5. Correction insert: 18.2 retracts and −18.19 lands; source words retained.
6. Giant 0.45 s: early Android-emulator recognition evidence; no subtitle over
   the number; readable qualification beneath. Not full bot response time.
7. Offline: local queue tiles hold their positions, approved delivery appears.
8. Integration: lateral track across context → voice → proposed answers → host.
9. Adaptation: forms / terminology / voices, then scoped domain fine-tuning.
10. Closing: echo the opening, one record and one pilot invitation.

Additional requested animation grammar: Channel Thread stages the spoken
question and answer before Capture Reveal prepares review. Reel Collage shows
the configured forms, vocabulary and voices; Announce Title closes the film.
All use original product artwork rather than their registry placeholders.

## Timing and sound
One JSON story and generated timing file drive scenes, subtitles, score, foley
and render checks. Scene lengths round up to two-second bars at 120 BPM and
never truncate speech. Confident British narration; distinguish the engineer and
assistant in the dialogue. Original 120 BPM plucked-synth/felt-mallet score, no
stock music. Forty-three timed field-entry pops, review/correction chimes,
reveal accents and transition sweeps. Music ducks smoothly beneath speech,
with additional separation for the engineer / assistant dialogue.
Two near-silent breaths before the metric reveal and closing. Voice stays clear
above the bed. Final two-pass loudness normalization targets −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP.
All speech is synthetic; no engineer microphone recording is used.

## Captions and evidence
English captions in a high-contrast bottom toast, outside the UI action area,
also exported as SRT. Long claims remain visible for reading. Illustrative
product animation is explicitly labelled; animation timing is not a benchmark.
The five-field form is the current short demo, not an approved operational form.
Visible labels describe speech recognition and voice synthesis without naming
model providers. Retain the measured ≈0.45 s recognition result and its scope;
do not substitute an unsupported 0.3 s claim.
Fine-tuning is a proposed scoped activity with approved data and validation,
not a claim that the shipped model was already fine-tuned. Integration is
proposed adapter work, not an already deployed Openreach integration.

## Review
Render key frames and a contact sheet, inspect all requested component scenes,
review the Player at desktop and 390px, check all media decode, captions, final
duration, no black frames, and measured audio loudness. Keep substantive scene
revisions inside one review round and preserve the original product film.
